Plan Lume’s narrated ad

You can write a visual promise and a nine-scene script.

An impressive sequence can hide the product. The script should carry one recognizable idea through the ending.

In 1 minute

  1. Message: a pause of light in a dark setting
  2. Structure: nine scenes in 18 seconds
  3. Final action: learn about the fictional concept

1Start the production plan with the message

Production plan brings together the message, scenes, and review criteria.

Mila, a 37-year-old director, helps Paulo, a 54-year-old shop owner, study six fictional ads.

The first introduces Lume, a magenta flashlight.

The idea is a pause of light in a dark setting.

The film makes no claims about the product’s reach, battery life, or durability.

Open a note and write the audience, message, and final action.

Example: people who appreciate design objects; discover the flashlight’s shape; learn about the concept.

The teaching ad will run 18 seconds and have nine scenes.

The full script is in the case workbook, with timings and each scene’s purpose.

Copy the nine scenes into your note and record their purpose.

Mila decides what must stay consistent before asking for variations.

Open the case script and sheet · View teaching references.

2An opening must still make sense later

The first three seconds introduce darkness, a beam, and the product.

Then the sequence explores texture and scale before returning to the flashlight.

The suggested narration is: When everything looks the same, a pause changes how you see it.

Lume: imagine another path.

It is a concept line, not a technical promise.

Use an image generator, a video generator, and an editor you already know how to use.

The Prompting, Kling, and CapCut courses provide that foundation.

Check your credits before starting.

This lesson’s practice ends with the script; you will make the files in later steps.

Paulo checks whether he can explain the ad in one sentence.

3Check the decision against its use

Define one message and give each scene a purpose tied to the flashlight.

Paulo uses the sheet to check the final action: learn about the fictional concept.

The goal is to make it feel like one continuous journey.

Open the kit’s instrumental track and listen to all 20 seconds.

Mark four segments: 0–5, 5–10, 10–15, and 15–20 seconds.

A smooth transition in a still image can break when the person changes scale or direction in motion.

In 1 minute

  1. Video: four segments in 20 seconds
  2. Transitions: body and architecture without unintended jumps
  3. Sound: one continuous track, with credits saved

1Generate and check one transition first

Use the approved pair with a start and end frame feature, if your account offers it.

Ask for a slow walk in the same corridor, with the camera following smoothly.

If only a starting image is available, generate a short segment and use its last clean frame to continue.

Do not promise a continuous journey before watching it.

Check feet, face, outfit, and pillars in the video.

Create the four segments with close transition points.

Save the approved files.

If a transition fails, redo it; a long dissolve can create two overlapping people.

2Music continues even when the image is split

Arrange the four clips over it and cut at the chosen transitions.

Small timing changes may help; sudden speed changes need another review.

Export the MP4 and watch it from beginning to end.

Record whether the journey feels continuous or has a visible cut.

Do not call four edited clips one real take.
